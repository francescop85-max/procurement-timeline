User Guide

Three planning tools to estimate procurement timelines, manage agricultural input campaigns, and plan LOA quality assurance processes — all respecting Ukrainian public holidays.

📦 Procurement Timeline 🌱 Agricultural Planner 📋 LOA QA Planner

SECTION 1

Overview of the Three Tools

ToolPurposePrimary user
Procurement Timeline Estimator Estimates procurement timelines working backward from a desired delivery date. Identifies when a PR must be raised. ROs initiating procurement

SECTION 2

Procurement Timeline Estimator

Given a PR date and desired delivery date, the tool works forward to show each procurement phase and whether the timeline is feasible. It also works backward to tell you the latest possible PR date for a given desired delivery.

Creating a timeline — step by step

1
Select a process type

Choose from the left panel (goods, services, works, framework contract). The tool auto-recommends a procurement method based on estimated value.

2
Enter the PR date

Set the date the PR will be (or was) submitted to procurement. This anchors the forward timeline.

3
Enter estimated value and desired dates

Set the estimated contract value. Then set the desired PO issuance date and desired delivery date. The tool shows whether the timeline is feasible (green), at risk (orange), or not feasible (red).

4
Review the Gantt chart

Each phase appears as a bar: solid = best case (min days), light = worst-case buffer (max days). Hover any bar for details.

5
Apply modifiers

Under "Additional Circumstances", check any conditions that apply (e.g. no-objection required, sole source justification). Each modifier adds days to the relevant phase.

6
Customize steps (optional)

Click ✏️ Customize Steps to add, remove, reorder, or adjust durations for individual phases. Click "Reset to defaults" to restore the standard process.

7
Share a monitoring link

Click Share Monitor Link, enter the PR number, then copy the generated URL. Anyone opening it sees the same timeline and can track actual progress against planned dates.

Status indicators

On track Today is before the earliest expected completion date.
At risk Today falls within the min–max completion range.
Overdue Today is past the latest expected completion date.

Monitoring view

When someone opens a shared monitoring link, the app switches to tracking mode. In this mode you can record actual completion dates for each phase. The Gantt updates in real time to show actual vs. planned progress.

Agricultural Input Planner

The Agricultural Input Planner works backward from a target planting or delivery date. For each campaign (e.g. spring wheat seeds, fertilizers), it calculates the latest possible PR date and displays all campaigns on a shared Gantt — making bottlenecks and scheduling conflicts immediately visible.

Adding a campaign — step by step

1
Click "+ New Campaign"

Opens the campaign panel on the right side of the screen.

2
Fill in campaign details
FieldWhat to enter
Crop / Input Typee.g. "Spring Wheat Seeds", "Urea Fertilizer"
Target Planting / Delivery DateDate by which inputs must reach beneficiaries
Estimated Value (USD)Used to auto-recommend a procurement method
Procurement TypeGoods, Services, or Works
Delivery Lead TimeWeeks the supplier needs to deliver after PO issuance
3
Review the recommended procurement method

The tool recommends a method based on value and type. You can override this if needed.

4
Add funding projects (optional but recommended)

Link the campaign to one or more projects with end dates. The tool flags if the procurement timeline risks missing a project deadline.

5
Apply modifiers and a custom step

Check applicable "Additional Circumstances". You can also insert a one-off custom step (e.g. a specific approval) at any position in the process.

6
Save

Click Add Campaign. The campaign appears in the shared Gantt timeline and the campaign table below it.

Reading the Gantt timeline

  • Each row represents one campaign.
  • The bars show the procurement window from PR deadline to delivery deadline.
  • A red dashed line marks today.
  • Click any bar to expand the campaign's individual phase breakdown.
  • Drag a phase bar to manually adjust timing — all dependent phases shift accordingly.

Campaign table

Below the Gantt, the table lists all campaigns with PR deadline, PO deadline, delivery deadline, funding project status, and quick Edit / Delete buttons.

Print / PDF

Click 🖨 Print / PDF in the header to generate a print-friendly view of the timeline and table.

SECTION 4

LOA QA Planner

⚠️
This tool covers only LoAs within FAO Ukraine's delegated authority (≤ USD 100,000). For LoAs above this amount, submit directly to the CSLP/LOA Unit.

The LOA QA Planner estimates the time required to complete the full quality assurance process for a Letter of Agreement — from documentation preparation through GRMS order approval. It covers both new LoAs and amendments, and automatically includes LPC review steps when the value exceeds the threshold.

Process types

ProcessWhen to use
New LoA — Direct SelectionSingle-source LoA with written justification
New LoA — Competitive (EOI)Open Expression of Interest to select a partner
New LoA — Competitive (IFP with scoring criteria)Invitation for Proposals with evaluation criteria; triggers LPC ex-ante if value > USD 25,000
Amendment — No CostExtension or scope change with no budget change
Amendment — Additional CostIncrease in LoA value
Amendment — Reduction of CostDecrease in LoA value

LPC review — when it applies

LPC review steps are automatically included when the LoA value exceeds USD 25,000.

  • For IFP processes above the threshold: two LPC stages — ex-ante (endorsement of scoring criteria before issuance) and ex-post (review after proposal evaluation).
  • For EOI and additional-cost amendments above the threshold: a single LPC review step is added.
  • At or below USD 25,000: LPC steps are removed automatically.

SECTION 5

Settings & Profiles

Access Settings via the ⚙️ button in the Procurement Timeline Estimator header. Settings are split into two tabs:

Procurement Profiles

Profiles let you configure different lead time sets for different operational contexts (e.g. different field offices, emergency vs. standard procedures).

  • Default profile — the baseline lead times used unless another profile is active. You can modify its lead times directly.
  • Creating a custom profile: click + New Profile, give it a name, set custom min/max durations per process step, then Save. The profile becomes available in both the Procurement Estimator and the Agricultural Planner.
  • Activating a profile: click Set Active. The active profile's lead times are used for all new timeline calculations.

LOA QA Settings

Configure lead times for each of the six LOA process types. Changes apply globally (there are no per-profile LOA settings).

  • LPC threshold — value above which LPC review steps are triggered. Default: USD 25,000.
  • Authority limit — maximum LoA value within FAO Ukraine's delegated authority. Default: USD 100,000.
  • To adjust step durations, expand a process type and set min/max working days per step.

Sharing & Monitoring Links

Procurement Timeline Estimator

  1. Configure the full timeline (process type, dates, modifiers).
  2. Click Share Monitor Link.
  3. Enter the PR number (required) and a label (optional).
  4. Click Generate & Copy Link — the URL is copied to your clipboard.
  5. Share via email or Teams.

Recipients open the link and see the timeline in tracking mode, where they can record actual completion dates for each phase.

LOA QA Planner

  1. Open the plan you want to share.
  2. Click 📤 Share Monitor Link in the plan header.
  3. The URL is copied immediately — share it directly.

Recipients opening the link are taken directly to that plan.

ℹ️
Procurement monitoring links are self-contained — the full plan snapshot is encoded in the URL itself, so recipients don't need an account or access to any database to view it.

SECTION 7

Quick Reference: Procurement Thresholds

Thresholds come from MS 502 Appendix G (effective 13 August 2026) and depend on the office tier. FAO Ukraine is a Decentralized Office with an IPO, so Table 2 applies. The tier is selectable in Settings — change it and every threshold, and the LPC trigger derived from it, moves with it. Bands are inclusive: a purchase of exactly USD 10,000 is a Micro Purchase, not an RFQ.

Procurement Committee review

The LPC Review Threshold is not a fixed number — Appendix F2 §2 defines it as the ITB/RFP value for the office, so for FAO Ukraine it is USD 50,000.

What actually escalates a competitive tender. An ITB or RFP reaches the RPC or HQPC when the award becomes Exceptional — most often because fewer than three responsive offers were received, or the award goes to other than the highest-ranked Vendor (MS 502.10.1.1.2). Tick “Fewer than 3 responsive offers” under Additional Circumstances to model this.

Procurement Authority — FAO Representative (Appendix C1)

FAO Ukraine’s IPO is at P3, which sets the FAO Representative’s limits. Above these an ad-hoc re-delegation must be obtained from the Regional Office or CSLP before tender issuance.

Emergency Situations and Exigencies

Committee review is never waived in an Emergency or Exigency, but it can change shape:

  • Email circulation — written comments from at least 5 committee members instead of a meeting (App. D2 §4 / E2 §4), or a “virtual meeting” authorised by the Chair (MS 502.9.3).
  • Ex post facto — submitted within 60 calendar days of contract issuance, subject to the Procurement Authority’s agreement (MS 502.9.9). This takes the review off the critical path. Missing the deadline is a breach of MS 502.
  • Never ex post: RFP evaluation criteria, and any procurement above USD 5m.
  • Under MS 502.6.7.6, procure by Direct Procurement only what is immediately required — the balance must go through a competitive process.

Committees meet at least twice a month (MS 502.9.3), so an LPC step can wait longer than the nominal 5 days when it misses a meeting.

LOA thresholds

Letters of Agreement are governed by MS 507 and are expressly excluded from MS 502 (Appendix B §5), so the Appendix G thresholds above do not apply to them.
